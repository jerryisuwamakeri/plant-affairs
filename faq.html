Plant Care Questions

Everything you need to know about keeping your plants healthy and thriving.

How often should I water my indoor plants?

Watering frequency depends on several factors including plant type, pot size, soil mix, light conditions, and season. As a general rule:

  • Succulents and cacti: Water every 2-4 weeks, allowing soil to dry completely between waterings
  • Tropical plants: Water when the top inch of soil feels dry, typically every 1-2 weeks
  • Ferns and moisture-loving plants: Keep soil consistently moist but not waterlogged
  • Most houseplants: Water when the top 1-2 inches of soil are dry

Always check your plant's specific needs and adjust based on your home environment. Overwatering is the most common cause of houseplant death.

Which plants are best for low light conditions?

Many plants can thrive in low light conditions. Here are some of the best options:

  • Snake Plant (Sansevieria): Extremely tolerant of low light and irregular watering
  • ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia): Thrives in low light and requires minimal care
  • Pothos (Epipremnum aureum): Adaptable to various light conditions, including low light
  • Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum): Prefers low to medium indirect light
  • Cast Iron Plant (Aspidistra elatior): As the name suggests, very hardy in low light
  • Chinese Evergreen (Aglaonema): Comes in many varieties, all tolerant of low light

Remember that "low light" doesn't mean no light. Even low-light plants need some natural light to survive.

How do I know if my plant needs repotting?

Look for these signs that your plant may need a larger container:

  • Roots growing through drainage holes or visible at the soil surface
  • Soil drying out very quickly after watering
  • Slowed growth despite proper care and growing season
  • Plant becoming top-heavy and tipping over easily
  • Salt and mineral buildup on the soil surface or pot edges
  • Water sitting on top of soil instead of absorbing quickly

The best time to repot is during the growing season (spring and summer). Choose a pot that is only 1-2 inches larger in diameter than the current one.

What's the best way to fertilize my plants?

Proper fertilization is key to healthy plant growth. Follow these guidelines:

  • Timing: Fertilize during the active growing season (spring and summer), reduce or stop in fall and winter
  • Frequency: Most houseplants benefit from monthly fertilization during growing season
  • Type: Use a balanced, water-soluble fertilizer (e.g., 10-10-10) for most plants, or specialized formulas for specific plant types
  • Application: Always water plants before fertilizing to prevent root burn
  • Dilution: Follow package instructions, and when in doubt, use half the recommended strength

Signs of over-fertilization include brown leaf tips, yellowing leaves, and salt buildup on soil surface. If you notice these, flush the soil with water to remove excess fertilizer.

What is your delivery process for live plants?

We take special care to ensure your plants arrive in perfect condition:

  • Professional packaging: Plants are securely potted and stabilized to prevent soil spillage
  • Protective wrapping: Delicate leaves and stems are protected with specialized plant sleeves
  • Climate control: During extreme weather, we use climate-controlled vehicles

Troubleshooting Questions

Solutions for common plant problems and health issues.

Why are my plant's leaves turning yellow?

Yellow leaves can indicate several different issues. Here's how to diagnose the problem:

  • Overwatering: Most common cause. Leaves turn yellow, may feel soft or mushy. Check soil moisture and reduce watering frequency.
  • Underwatering: Leaves turn yellow and crispy, starting from tips. Soil is very dry. Increase watering frequency.
  • Nutrient deficiency: Older leaves turn yellow while veins remain green. Apply balanced fertilizer.
  • Too much direct sun: Leaves yellow and may have brown spots. Move plant to location with indirect light.
  • Pest infestation: Check for tiny insects on leaves and stems. Treat with appropriate insecticide.
  • Natural aging: Older leaves naturally yellow and drop as plant grows new ones. This is normal.

Identify the pattern of yellowing and check your watering habits first. Most yellow leaf issues are related to water management.

How do I deal with common plant pests?

Here are natural solutions for common houseplant pests:

  • Spider mites: Tiny red or brown mites, webbing on leaves. Wipe leaves with damp cloth, spray with neem oil solution, increase humidity.
  • Mealybugs: White cottony masses in leaf joints. Dab with cotton swab dipped in rubbing alcohol, then treat with insecticidal soap.
  • Scale: Brown or tan bumps on stems and leaves. Scrape off gently, treat with horticultural oil.
  • Aphids: Small green or black insects on new growth. Spray with strong water stream, use insecticidal soap.
  • Fungus gnats: Small black flies around soil. Let soil dry between waterings, use yellow sticky traps, apply beneficial nematodes.

For severe infestations, isolate the affected plant to prevent spreading. Always test treatments on a small area first. Prevention through proper care is the best defense against pests.

What causes brown leaf tips and how can I fix it?

Brown leaf tips are usually caused by environmental factors rather than disease:

  • Low humidity: Most common cause. Use a humidifier, pebble tray, or mist plants regularly.
  • Over-fertilization: Salt buildup burns leaf tips. Flush soil with water to remove excess salts.
  • Underwatering: Consistent dry soil causes tips to brown. Establish a regular watering schedule.
  • Water quality: Chemicals in tap water can cause tip burn. Use filtered or distilled water.
  • Root bound: Constricted roots can't take up enough water. Repot into larger container.

You can trim the brown tips with clean scissors, following the natural shape of the leaf. Address the underlying cause to prevent further browning.
